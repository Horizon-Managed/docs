---
title: "Client reschedule requests"
description: "Let a client propose a new date for their upcoming alignment review from the portal, and approve or decline it from the Schedule dashboard"
---

A client can't move their own alignment review — but from the portal they can
**request** a reschedule, and your team approves or declines it. The request lands
in an approval queue on the Summit [Schedule dashboard](/composer/alignment-reviews),
a manager or vCIO decides, and the client sees the outcome back in their portal.
Nothing about the schedule changes until someone on your side approves it.

## The client's request

From their [portal alignment page](/composer/portal-alignment), a client with an
upcoming review sees a **Request reschedule** action. It opens a small form where
they propose a **new date and time** and, optionally, a **reason** for the move.

- The proposed time must be in the future.
- The request is tied to *their own* company's scheduled review — a client can
  only ever propose against their own schedule, and the company and requester
  identity come from their portal session, never from the form.
- Requests are rate-limited, so a client can't flood the queue.

Once submitted, the request shows on the client's own alignment page as
**Pending**, alongside any earlier requests and their outcomes — so the client
always has a record of what they asked for and how it was decided.

## The staff decision

Pending requests surface at the top of the Summit **Schedule dashboard** so
they're the first thing your team sees, and they're visible in the TAM workspace.
Each request shows the client, the standard, the currently-scheduled date, the
client's proposed date, and their reason.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the request">
    Open the Schedule dashboard. The client reschedule-requests queue lists every
    pending request with the current date, the proposed date, and the client's
    reason.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve">
    Approving **moves the review** to the client's proposed date and marks the
    request approved. The client sees the new date and an approved status back in
    their portal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decline">
    Declining leaves the scheduled date **unchanged**. You can attach an optional
    note explaining why — the client sees that note next to the declined request
    in their portal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Who can approve

Seeing the queue and deciding on it are separate permissions. A **TAM** can see
pending requests on the Schedule dashboard, but **approving or declining requires
a manager, vCIO, or admin**. A user without approval permission who tries to
decide is refused.

Every request and every decision is written to a tenant-scoped **audit trail** —
who requested it, who decided it, and when — so the whole exchange is
reconstructable.

<Note>
  Approving is idempotent and safe against races: a request that's already been
  decided can't be approved or declined a second time, and if the underlying
  schedule was deactivated between the request and the approval, the move is
  rejected rather than silently applied. The client is notified in-portal (they
  see the updated status on their alignment page); a separate email notification
  is planned.
</Note>
