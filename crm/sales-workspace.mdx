The Sales Workspace is the home screen for a salesperson in **NEXOS CORE**. It
pulls everything you work on into one view: a **goal strip** across the top that
shows how you are tracking against your number, a rail of every deal you own down
the left, and — in the center — the **NexOS C²** panel for the deal you have
focused, where you set its economics and timeline. A per-deal timer and a live
activity feed sit on the right.

Open it at `/nexiq/workspace`.

Everything on the page revolves around one **focused deal**. Click a deal in the
left rail (or search for it) and the center panel, the timer, and the activity
feed all follow it.

## When to use it

- Start your day here to see your attainment, pace, and commissions at a glance.
- Work a single deal end to end — update its numbers, timeline, and next step
  without leaving the page.
- Capture a brand-new lead from a call transcript and let Nexie fill in the
  details.
- Track time against the exact deal you are working on.
- Keep the **Sales Cockpit** current for your manager without any extra data
  entry.

## The goal strip

The sticky strip across the top is your scoreboard. It stays visible as you
scroll and is broken into five zones.

| Zone | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **My Sales Goal** | Your name and the current goal period, with days remaining. |
| **Attainment** | Closed dollars so far, a progress bar toward your goal, the percentage attained, and the remaining gap. |
| **Revenue Mix** | Your MRR, ORR, and NRR each against its own target, plus **Project Mix** — non-recurring revenue as a percentage of MRR, measured against the 20% benchmark. |
| **Pace** | Whether you are on track, the dollars-per-day you need to close to hit your goal, and your average days to close. |
| **Commissions** | Commission **earned** to date and **forecast** on your open pipeline. |

Your goal, revenue-mix targets, and commission target are set by your manager in
the **Plan Cascade** — you see them here, you don't edit them.

<Note>
  **Project Mix** compares your non-recurring (project / break-fix) revenue to
  your recurring revenue. The 20% target follows the TruMethods guideline: a
  healthy book carries roughly one dollar of project work for every five dollars
  of managed services. The figure turns amber when you fall below it.
</Note>

## The deal rail

The left rail is your whole pipeline, list-style. Deals are grouped into
**prospects**, **suspects**, and **closed**, each with a count. A search box
filters the list by company name (or deal title), and the header shows your
totals — number of deals, total MRR, and weighted value (MRR × close
probability).

Two buttons sit at the top of the rail:

- **New Deal** — opens the standard deal-create form. This is the same form and
  the same save path the pipeline kanban uses, so a deal you create here shows up
  everywhere. When it saves, the rail refreshes and focuses the new deal.
- **New Lead (from transcript)** — opens the transcript-driven lead capture flow
  (see [Capture a lead from a transcript](#capture-a-lead-from-a-transcript)).

A link at the bottom opens the full [pipeline kanban](/crm/index) if you'd rather
drag deals between stages.

## The NexOS C² deal panel

The center of the page is **NexOS C²** — the source-of-truth inputs for the
focused deal. A **LIVE → Cockpit** badge in the header signals that every change
you make here streams straight to the shared **Sales Cockpit** your manager
watches. There is no separate save button; edits are saved automatically a moment
after you finish typing.

The panel is organized into sections so the fields you touch daily stay front and
center.

### Identity

Company, sales rep (read-only — it's you), and the **PBR bucket** the deal sits
in (30-, 60-, 90-, or 180-day prospect, cold, suspect A/B/C, or closed
won/lost).

### Economics

This is where the deal's money is set. **Seats** and **AISP** are two separate
inputs, and together they drive the recurring value:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a product (optional)">
    Selecting a product from your catalog prefills **AISP** with that product's
    per-seat monthly price. It's a starting point — you can still edit it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter seats and AISP">
    **Seats** is the headcount on the deal. **AISP** (Average Initial Sale Price)
    is the price *per seat, per month*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="MRR and ARR calculate for you">
    **MRR** is read-only and always equals **AISP × Seats**. **ARR** is also
    read-only and equals **MRR × 12**. Change either seats or AISP and both
    recompute instantly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For example, 45 seats at an AISP of $200 gives an MRR of $9,000 and an ARR of
$108,000. A **Close Probability %** field rounds out the section and feeds the
weighted value shown in the rail and the goal strip.

<Note>
  AISP is a convenience input — the deal stores the resulting **MRR**. If you
  clear seats or AISP, MRR falls back to empty rather than showing a stale
  number.
</Note>

### Stage & timeline

The expected **close date**, the deal **source** (referral, COI, marketing,
outbound, inbound, event), and the **last step** and **next step** with their
dates. The next-step date is color-coded so an overdue follow-up stands out.

**Close Date**, **Last Step Date** and **Next Step Date** each open a calendar
when you click them, rather than accepting typed text. Pick a day and the field
saves itself the same way every other field on the panel does — there is no
separate save step, and the change reaches the **Sales Cockpit** immediately.

<Note>
  These are **calendar dates**, not moments in time. A date you pick reads back
  as exactly that day for everyone who looks at the deal, whatever time zone
  they're in, and re-opening the calendar lands you on the day already stored so
  you can nudge it without re-selecting from scratch.
</Note>

### Admin & system fields

Collapsed by default so the everyday fields have room to breathe. Expand it to
set **Entered PBR** — the date the deal entered the PBR, picked from the same
calendar and saved the same way — and its PBR section; the deal's **stage** is
shown here read-only (it moves as the deal advances through the pipeline, not by
editing this field).

For details on how these numbers roll up into revenue reporting, see
[contract coverage & profitability](/crm/contract-coverage) and the
[product catalog](/product/index).

## Working tabs

Below the C² panel, a set of tabs holds the rest of the deal's context —
**Overview**, **Notes & SRS**, **Quotes**, **Documents**, **Emails**, and **My
Time**.

The **My Time** tab shows your week-to-date hours pulled from the shared
timesheet and grouped by sales category (discovery, quote building, follow-up,
proposal prep, and so on), plus a per-deal breakdown so you can see which deals
are earning your hours.

## The deal timer

On the right, the **Deal Timer** tracks time against whichever deal you have
focused. It shows the deal name, a running clock, the current category, and
Start / Pause / Stop controls.

- **Categories** cycle through Discovery Call, Demo, Proposal Prep, Follow-up,
  Negotiation, and Admin — use the gear button to switch.
- **Change** (or clicking a deal in the rail) re-points the timer at a different
  deal. When you switch, the timer **pauses** whatever was running and picks up
  the target deal's own saved time — it never merges two deals' hours.
- Each deal keeps its own running total, so you can pause and step away to
  another deal, then come back and the elapsed time is still there. (Pressing
  **Stop** clears the running total for that deal.)

<Note>
  The Deal Timer runs in your browser and keeps each deal's elapsed time locally.
  It's a focus aid for the deal in front of you; your official hours live on your
  timesheet and in the **My Time** tab.
</Note>

## The activity feed

Under the timer, a live feed lists the deal's recent history — stage changes
(including the move to won or lost), timer starts, imported transcripts,
generated quotes, meetings held, emails, and calls — newest first, so you have
the full story of the deal without leaving the page. Stage changes are recorded
whether you move the deal by hand or an automation rule moves it for you. Below it,
**Time Today** totals your hours across deals, and a **Nexie** panel offers a
suggestion for your next best move.

## Capture a lead from a transcript

The **New Lead** flow turns a raw call transcript into ready-to-save records.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open New Lead">
    Click **New Lead (from transcript)** in the deal rail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drop in a transcript">
    Upload a `.txt` or `.md` file, or paste text from Plaud, Otter, Granola, or
    any source. Click **Analyze with Nexie**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review what Nexie extracted">
    Nexie pulls out the contact, the interaction, pain points, and quotable
    opportunities, and shows you the AI cost of the run. Nothing is saved yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm and save">
    Adjust anything that needs it, choose whether to also create an opportunity
    in your pipeline, and save. You confirm every field before it's written.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Transcript analysis uses Nexie, which needs your tenant's Claude API key
  configured. The extracted values are always yours to review and edit before
  anything is saved.
</Note>

## How it connects

- **New Deal** and **New Lead** write through the same paths as the
  [CRM pipeline](/crm/index), so deals and contacts you create here appear
  everywhere.
- The **NexOS C²** panel streams its fields to the **Sales Cockpit**, giving your
  manager a live read of your pipeline with no double entry.
- **Product** selection pulls per-seat pricing from your
  [product catalog](/product/index); revenue is classified as MRR, ORR, or NRR
  and reported through [contract coverage & profitability](/crm/contract-coverage).
