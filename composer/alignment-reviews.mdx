A **Technology Alignment review** measures how well a client's environment matches
a best-practice [Standard](/composer/index#standards-vs-compliance-frameworks) —
the AI Baseline, the Core Infrastructure Baseline, or one you authored. Unlike a
compliance audit, it's advisory: you judge each control, turn the gaps into a
costed roadmap, and walk the client through the result to a signature. Open it at
`/summit/reviews`.

The review runs as a **guided, gated workflow**. A cyan stepper across the top of
the workspace lights up as you progress, and each stage unlocks only when the
previous one is genuinely finished — you can't sign off a half-judged review, and
the review isn't complete until the client has signed.

## The guided workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Judge controls">
    Mark each control **Aligned**, **At risk**, **Not aligned**, or **N/A**. The
    step is done when every control has a judgment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build recommendations">
    Turn every misaligned finding into a disposition — promote it to the client
    **roadmap**, or **Accept Risk & Defer** it. Unlocks once all controls are
    judged.
  </Step>
  <Step title="TAM sign-off">
    The Technical Account Manager signs off the technical assessment. Unlocks only
    when every control is judged **and** every misaligned finding is
    dispositioned.
  </Step>
  <Step title="vCIO countersign">
    A second reviewer (the vCIO) reviews the TAM's work and countersigns — or
    flags controls and returns the review to the TAM for revision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Client walkthrough">
    Hand the client the review on their own device to walk the findings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Client sign-off">
    The client signs off, which seals the review as complete. There is no other
    way to complete a review.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each stage's badge shows **done**, **active**, or **pending**, and a persistent
action strip at the foot of the control list keeps the single next step reachable
no matter how long the control list is.

## Judging controls

For every control, record a judgment. There's no evidence to upload — alignment is
a professional judgment, so it moves fast.

- **List / Cards view.** Toggle between a full-width **List** and a roomier
  **Cards** layout; your choice is remembered per user. Both show a
  judgment-colored accent so the posture reads at a glance.
- **Per-control notes with voice dictation.** Add a note to any control — type it,
  or tap the microphone to **dictate** it. Notes are where the "why" lives for the
  client conversation, and they carry through to the recommendation text and the
  final report.
- **Live score.** The alignment score updates as you go — Aligned = full credit,
  At risk = half, Not aligned = none; N/A is excluded from the score entirely.
  Progress tracks how many of the total controls you've judged.

## Building recommendations

Every control judged **Not aligned** or **At risk** becomes a finding you must
dispose of before the review can be signed off. For each one you either **Promote**
it to the roadmap or **Accept Risk & Defer** it — the TAM sign-off is blocked until
none are left undecided.

- **Promote to the roadmap.** Set a **priority**, an **estimated effort**, and a
  **cost**, and push the finding onto the client's
  [Technology Roadmap](/composer/technology-roadmap) as a planned item. Each
  promoted item keeps its provenance — the Standard and control it came from.
- **Accept Risk & Defer.** When the client chooses not to remediate a finding,
  record the deferral instead: the client's **initials**, a short
  **justification**, and a required **re-review date**. On a tablet the client
  **draws their initials** (finger or Apple Pencil) and picks the re-review date on
  a large, touch-friendly calendar. Accepting a risk is a *decision*, not a passing
  grade — it doesn't change the alignment score, and the finding stays technically
  not-aligned and resurfaces for re-review when the date arrives.

The score header reflects both, e.g. **"72% aligned · 3 risks accepted."**

## Real sign-offs at every gate

The TAM, the vCIO, and the client each **sign** — not just click a button. Each
internal gate opens a signature pad; a sign-off is rejected unless it carries an
actual drawn signature, so a stage can never advance unsigned. The captured
signatures appear on the printable [Executive Summary](/composer/executive-summary).

- **TAM sign-off** requires the TAM role (a manager or admin may act in their
  place) and can only be recorded once every control is judged and every finding is
  dispositioned.
- **vCIO countersign** requires the vCIO role (or a manager/admin) and can only be
  recorded after the TAM has signed.
- **Client sign-off** happens on the client's own device during the walkthrough and
  is the only path to a completed review.

## Attestation integrity — locking and the vCIO round-trip

Once the TAM signs off, the assessment **freezes**: judgments, notes, and
dispositions become read-only, and the staff edit actions are refused until the
review is reopened. The client walkthrough is unaffected.

The vCIO review is a genuine two-way gate, modeled on a code review:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Flag controls">
    During the vCIO review the reviewer can **flag** individual controls with a
    comment. The vCIO never edits the TAM's judgment — separation of duties: the
    TAM owns the assessment, the vCIO annotates it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Countersign or return">
    The vCIO either **countersigns** — which is blocked while any flag is still
    open — or chooses **Return to TAM**. A return reopens the review, invalidates
    the TAM's sign-off, and bumps a revision round, while keeping the flags and
    comments visible so the TAM sees exactly what to fix.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reopen (recoverability)">
    A manager or admin can **Reopen** any signed-off review that the client hasn't
    yet signed — a general escape hatch for a mistaken sign-off.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  The full TAM ↔ vCIO back-and-forth is **auditable**. Every sign-off, flag,
  return, and reopen is written to a tenant-scoped audit trail on the same
  transaction as the state change, so it can never land unrecorded. It surfaces as
  a collapsible **Review activity** panel on the review page and a **Review
  history** section on the Executive Summary — with the actor, the timestamp, and
  the revision round for each event.
</Info>

## Pause and resume

A review can be explicitly **paused** ("Pause — finish later") and picked back up
whenever you're ready — progress is already auto-saved, so pausing just marks a
clear stopping point. A paused review shows an amber **Paused** badge with a
**Resume** action in the Reviews list, which distinguishes it from **In progress**
and **Complete** reviews at a glance. Pause and resume are recorded to the audit
trail alongside cancel.

## The deliverable

The review generates a branded, printable
[Executive Summary](/composer/executive-summary) for the client — the alignment
score, key findings, prioritized roadmap, the accepted-risk register, the sign-off
block with every captured signature, and the review history.

<Note>
  This alignment review is the technical (TAM/vCIO) motion. Promote its findings
  onto the client's plan with
  [Recommendations & the technology roadmap](/composer/technology-roadmap), hand it
  to the client on an iPad with the
  [client walkthrough](/composer/client-walkthrough), and layer the financial view
  on top with the [vCIO budget & QBR](/composer/vcio-qbr). The cross-client TAM
  **workspace** (`/summit/workspace`) and **recurring review scheduling** are both
  live.
</Note>
