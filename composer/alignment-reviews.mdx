---
title: "Technology Alignment reviews"
description: "Assess a client against a best-practice Standard, judge each control, and turn the gaps into a prioritized technology roadmap"
---

A **Technology Alignment review** measures how well a client's environment matches
a best-practice [Standard](/composer/index#standards-vs-compliance-frameworks) —
the AI Baseline, the Core Infrastructure Baseline, or one you authored. Unlike a
compliance audit, it's advisory: you judge each control and the gaps become a
costed roadmap you take to the client. Open it at `/summit/reviews`.

## Running a review

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a review">
    Pick the client and the alignment Standard. Re-opening the same pair resumes
    the review in progress rather than starting a duplicate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Judge each control">
    For every control, mark it **Aligned**, **At risk**, **Not aligned**, or
    **N/A**. There's no evidence to upload — alignment is a professional judgment,
    so it moves fast.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Capture context">
    Add a note to any control — type it, or use the microphone to dictate. Notes
    are where the "why" lives for the client conversation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch the score">
    The alignment score updates live as you go (Aligned = full credit, At risk =
    half, Not aligned = none; N/A is excluded). Progress tracks how many controls
    you've judged.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete">
    Finalize the review to lock the score and hand off to recommendations.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## From gaps to recommendations

Every control marked **Not aligned** or **At risk** surfaces as a recommendation
candidate. For each, set a **priority** (critical / high / medium / low), an
**estimated effort** (hours), and a **cost**. These are the improvements you'll
propose to the client.

## Promote to the roadmap

Promote a recommendation to push it onto the client's **Technology Roadmap** as a
planned item. Each promoted item keeps its **provenance** — which Standard and
control it came from — so the roadmap always shows *why* the work is there. A
control can't be promoted twice; if it's already on the roadmap, Summit points you
at the existing item instead of creating a duplicate.

From the roadmap you can edit an item, change its status as work progresses, or
remove it. Promoted items appear on the client's roadmap alongside anything added
by hand.

## Risk acceptance & sign-off

Not every gap gets remediated. When a client chooses to **accept a risk** rather
than fix a misaligned control, record it right on the finding — the client's
**initials**, a short **justification**, and a **re-review date** (accepted risks
don't disappear; they resurface for re-review when that date arrives). Accepting a
risk is a *decision*, not a passing grade: it doesn't change the alignment score,
and accepted risks are shown distinctly (e.g. "72% aligned · 3 risks accepted").

At the end, **sign off** to seal the review — it's marked complete, every accepted
risk is finalized, and the signer is recorded. Each acceptance and sign-off is
written to a tenant-scoped audit trail for internal and external review.
