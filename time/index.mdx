Time tracking has two connected layers. **Live timers** are running stopwatches
you start against a ticket, a project task, or a site survey — several can run
at once, with one "focused" in the header and the rest ticking in the
background. Stopping a timer writes a time entry. **Timesheets** are the weekly
rollup of those entries: review the week, submit it, a manager approves, and
approved sheets push to QuickBooks or export to CSV.

Timesheets live at `/timesheets`. Timers run from the app header, and from the
thing you're timing — see [where you can start one](#where-you-can-start-one)
below.

## When to use it

- Track live hours against a ticket, project task, or survey while you work.
- Run several jobs at once and switch which is focused, without losing the
  others' elapsed time.
- Start on your phone and keep going on your desktop, or the other way around.
- Capture billable time — with a work type, billing role, contract, and
  effective rate when the timer is on a ticket.
- Review, submit, and approve a week of time.
- Push approved time to QuickBooks or export it for payroll.

## From timer to bill

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a timer">
    Start against a ticket, task, or survey. Timers are **server-side and
    follow you across devices** — one started on your phone shows up on your
    desktop, focused the same way, and vice versa. If that item already has a
    timer running, starting it again just **resumes** the existing one instead
    of creating a second timer for the same job. Starting a new timer unfocuses
    whichever one was focused before; the others keep accumulating time in the
    background.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop and capture billing context">
    On a **ticket**, stop asks you to confirm the work type, billing role,
    contract, and product (or enter a manual minute override); time draws down
    contract block-hours or retainer coverage automatically and records an
    effective rate. On a **task** or **survey** timer, stopping still writes a
    time entry, but at your tenant's default rate — there's no contract to
    resolve outside a ticket, so that time isn't attached to a specific
    contract line. A linked project task's actual hours are updated either way.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It becomes a time entry">
    The stop writes a unified time entry (with hours, billable amount, and cost)
    — and a ticket time entry too, when the timer was on a ticket.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Roll up, submit, approve">
    Entries aggregate into your Monday–Sunday timesheet. Submit it; an approver
    approves or rejects (with a reason).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Push or export">
    Approved timesheets push to QuickBooks Online and can be exported to CSV for
    payroll.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Where you can start one

The [mobile app's](/mobile/index) **Time Capture** tab offers every kind of work
you can put a timer on, grouped by type:

| Context | Where |
| --- | --- |
| **Tickets** — your open tickets | Ticket peek/detail on desktop, or Time Capture on mobile |
| **Work orders** — the ones you lead | Time Capture on mobile |
| **Project tasks** | Time Capture on mobile |
| **Projects** | Time Capture on mobile |
| **Deals** — the ones you own | The deal's card in [Sales Workspace](/crm/sales-workspace), or Time Capture on mobile |
| **Site surveys** — the ones you're running | A [survey's](/survey/index) detail page on desktop, or Time Capture on mobile |

Each option names its record the way you would say it out loud — `#24 — Printer
offline`, `WO-1043 — Cable pull, floor 3`, `Northwind Ltd — Managed services
renewal` — rather than showing a bare title.

The picker is a full-screen sheet of large buttons rather than a dropdown, and
separates "nothing is assigned to you" from "we could not load your work" — the
second offers a retry.

## Logging time by hand

Not every hour comes from a running timer. **Log Time** on a ticket records
hours after the fact, and asks for the **work type** and **billing role** — the
two things that decide what the hour bills at.

The modal shows what the hour will bill at before you commit it: the contract it
is priced against, the effective rate, and the amount. When it will not bill at
all, it says so plainly.

Manual entries price through the same code the timer stop uses, so the two ways
of recording an hour bill identically, and the contract is inherited from the
ticket.

Where nothing can price an hour — no work type and no billing role means there
is genuinely no rate — the entry is written **unpriced** rather than as a zero,
and surfaced: the invoice form lists those hours above the line items, and a
QuickBooks push that leaves them behind records them in the
[Sync Log](/integrations/sync-log). An entry you marked non-billable is a
correct zero and is never flagged.

<Tip>
  If your tenant has no work types or billing roles configured, the modal tells
  you and points at **Settings → Billing** rather than showing an empty picker
  that quietly produces unbillable time.
</Tip>

## Timesheets

Your timesheet (`/timesheets`) is the weekly view; approvers work a queue at
`/timesheets/approvals`, and an HR view with CSV export is available to users
with the right permission.

## Good to know

- **The label sticks with the timer, mostly.** A running ticket timer shows the
  ticket's current title, so if you rename the ticket mid-timer the label
  updates with it (unless the ticket is later deleted, in which case it falls
  back to the title it had when the timer started). Task and survey timers
  always show the title captured when the timer started, even if that item is
  later renamed.
- **Stopping a ticket timer can set the ticket's status**, and the picker offers
  **your own statuses** from Settings → Helpdesk — including any you classified
  as pausing the SLA clock.
- **A phone-stopped timer needs its billing context set before it can bill.**
  The mobile stop writes the same rows as the desktop stop, through the same
  code — but the phone does not yet ask for a work type and billing role, and
  without both there is no rate to resolve. Those hours arrive **unpriced**.
  Set the work type and billing role on the entry from the desktop, or log the
  time from the ticket, and it prices normally. Capturing billing context on
  the phone is planned.
- **Submitting a timesheet requires it to be yours.** Anyone with
  `timesheets:approve` can still submit an absent colleague's week so payroll can
  close it out; without that permission you can only submit your own.
- **QuickBooks push is optional and best-effort** — if it isn't configured or a
  push fails, the timesheet stays **Approved** (not Pushed) and never blocks
  approval; the error is recorded for retry and appears in the
  [Sync Log](/integrations/sync-log).
- Captured time is floored to a small minimum (a fraction of an hour), so very
  short timers still record.
