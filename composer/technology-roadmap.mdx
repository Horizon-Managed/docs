---
title: "Recommendations & the technology roadmap"
description: "Turn a review's misaligned controls into prioritized recommendations, then promote them onto the client's technology roadmap with full provenance"
---

An alignment review measures the gaps; the **technology roadmap** is what you do
about them. Every control a review judges **Not aligned** or **At risk** becomes a
recommendation candidate you can price, prioritize, and promote onto the client's
roadmap — and each promoted item remembers exactly which Standard and control it
came from. Open a review's recommendations at
`/summit/reviews/{id}/recommendations`, and the roadmap at `/summit/roadmap`.

## From findings to recommendations

Only misaligned controls surface as candidates — controls marked **Aligned** or
**N/A** never produce a recommendation. For each candidate, Summit proposes a
starting **priority** derived from the control's severity: a hard miss (Not
aligned) inherits the control's severity, and an At-risk finding is set one notch
less urgent. You override it before promoting.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open recommendations">
    From a completed or in-progress review, open **Recommendations**. Each
    misaligned control is listed with its severity, the "why it matters" text, and
    the note you captured during the review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set priority, effort, and cost">
    For each candidate set a **priority** (critical / high / medium / low), an
    **estimated effort** in hours, and an **estimated cost**. The recommendation's
    text is composed for you from the control — its why-it-matters, your review
    note, and the effort estimate — so there's nothing to write from scratch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Promote to the roadmap">
    Promote the candidate to push it onto the client's technology roadmap as a
    **planned** item. A control can't be promoted twice — if it's already on the
    roadmap, Summit points you at the existing item instead of creating a
    duplicate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A finding whose risk the client has **accepted** shows that disposition (with
  its re-review date) instead of re-offering promotion. Accepting a risk is a
  decision, not a fix — see
  [Technology Alignment reviews](/composer/alignment-reviews#risk-acceptance--sign-off).
</Note>

## Provenance — the roadmap always shows *why*

Every promoted item keeps a link back to the **Standard and control** it came
from. On the roadmap that link renders as a provenance chip, so anyone looking at
the roadmap can see which best-practice gap justifies the work. Provenance is
resolved server-side from the review — it can't be forged or edited — which is
what lets the roadmap double as the evidence trail for a vCIO conversation.

Items added to the roadmap by hand simply carry no provenance chip; promoted and
hand-added items live on the same roadmap.

## Managing the roadmap

Open `/summit/roadmap` and pick a client to see their roadmap. Items are ordered
with completed work last, then by priority, then newest first. For each item you
can:

- **Edit** its title, description, **category**, priority, **target quarter**, and
  estimated cost.
- **Change status** as work moves: `planned` → `in_progress` → `completed`, or
  `deferred` / `cancelled`. Marking an item completed stamps its completion date.
- **Remove** an item.

Categories are `infrastructure`, `security`, `compliance`, `software`,
`hardware`, `cloud`, or `other` — anything else collapses to `other`. Items
promoted from a review start in the `security` category and `planned` status; edit
them to fit.

<Note>
  The same roadmap items render **read-only** on the CRM client-detail
  Account-Management tab, so account managers see the plan without needing the
  Summit workspace. The **target quarter** you set here is what the vCIO
  [budget rollup](/composer/vcio-qbr) buckets investment by.
</Note>
