A substantial week on the ticket queue — a new column that answers a question
status never could, and the return of saved layouts that had been quietly broken
since May. Plus progress-at-a-glance on alignment reviews, and a better first
impression on the page your clients' clients actually see.

## The ticket queue knows who owes the next move

The queue has a new **Waiting On** column. Status tells you where a ticket sits in
your workflow; Waiting On tells you **who is currently blocking it** — **Us**,
**Customer**, **Vendor**, **Parts**, **Scheduled**, or **Working**.

The important part is that it maintains itself. It moves as the conversation moves,
so it never depends on a technician remembering to set a field:

- A **customer reply** hands the ticket back to you.
- A **public reply from a technician** hands it to the customer.
- **Internal notes and automation are inert** — talking amongst yourselves does not
  change who owes the next move.
- **Resolved and closed tickets are skipped.** Nobody owes a move on a finished
  ticket.

You can filter on it from the filter drawer, or directly with `?activity=` on the
queue URL.

### The Waiting tile now counts what its label says

The **Waiting** decision tile used to count tickets with the **Pending** status,
which is a different claim than the label made. Pending means a technician paused
the SLA clock; it says nothing about who is blocking. That produced two wrong
readings in opposite directions:

- A ticket parked on a Dell RMA was **Pending**, so it counted as "waiting on the
  customer" when it was waiting on a vendor.
- A ticket left **Open** after a technician replied — the common case, and the one
  you most want to see — did not count at all.

The tile now reads the Waiting On column, so it means what it says.

## Saved views, and layouts that stay put

**Saved views** are new: name a set of filters and return to it. Keep a view
private, or share it with the team so everyone triages from the same definition,
and mark one as your default so the queue opens on it. A view stores the filters
rather than a frozen list, so it re-runs and stays current every time you open it.

**Your column and density choices now persist** per user, across sessions and
devices. This is a repair as much as a feature: the setting had been saving to a
place that no longer existed since May, so layouts survived only in the browser
you set them in and vanished on any other machine. Hide a column now, come back
tomorrow on a different computer, and it is still hidden.

<Note>
  Fixing that surfaced a second, larger problem: an internal helper that every page
  in the product used to send authenticated requests was **undefined everywhere**,
  meaning the restored persistence could not have worked in a browser at all. Four
  existing features depended on the same helper. That is fixed too, so those are
  more reliable as a side effect.
</Note>

## Device column on the queue

A new **Device** column shows the RMM-managed machine a ticket concerns, so
endpoint problems can be triaged from the list. It is **hidden by default**, since
a ticket only carries a device once one has been linked — turn it on from
**Columns**.

Relatedly, the linked-asset panel in the ticket Peek is populating correctly again.
Its compliance and hardware tiles had been reading zero, and **failing** compliance
checks were invisible in the pill row — the exact checks the row exists to show.
The remote-desktop and shell actions on that panel also work where a device is
linked, instead of reporting no agent.

See [Ticket queue](/helpdesk/ticket-queue) and [Ticket Peek](/helpdesk/ticket-peek).

## Alignment reviews show their step from the list

Rows in the Reviews list now show which step of the guided rail each review is on —
for example **`2/5 · Build recommendations`** — so you can tell an untouched review
from one waiting on a vCIO countersign without opening either. The step is derived
from the same rules the workspace stepper uses, so the list cannot disagree with the
review it links to.

Inside a review, the rail also **updates as you work**: judge the last control and
the next stage unlocks in front of you, with no page reload needed to find out
whether a gate has opened.

See [Technology Alignment reviews](/composer/alignment-reviews).

## Client quote approval page

The quote approval page is the one page in the product a **client's own client** may
see. It now carries your branding rather than presenting a bare form, the signer's
name, title, and email arrive prefilled from the contact on the quote instead of
being retyped, and its confirmations are in-page dialogs styled to match — not the
browser's native alert boxes, which render unbranded and read as a phishing risk to
a cautious signer.

See [Quotes & invoices](/billing/index#online-approval--e-signature).

## Bug fixes

- The **Waiting** queue tile counted paused tickets rather than tickets waiting on a
  customer (as above).
- Per-user column and density persistence had been a silent no-op since May (as
  above).
- The shared helper used to send authenticated requests was undefined on every page,
  affecting four existing features (as above).
- The ticket Peek's linked-asset panel reported zeroed compliance and hardware
  tiles, hid failing compliance checks, and reported no linked agent for remote
  actions.
- The alignment review rail did not refresh as judgments landed.
