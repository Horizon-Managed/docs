Where the TAM judges the environment and builds the roadmap, the **vCIO** puts the
financial layer on top: a per-client IT budget, a roll-up of the roadmap against
that budget, and a client-facing **Quarterly Business Review (QBR)** that ties the
alignment score, the investment plan, and the accepted risks into one deliverable.
Open it at `/summit/qbr`.

## Set the client's IT budget

Summit keeps a per-client **annual IT budget** figure, set inline from the vCIO
panel. It's a Summit setting of its own — distinct from your MSP contract value or
the client's company revenue — so the roll-up measures the roadmap against what the
client actually has to spend. You can add a short fiscal note alongside it.

## Budget rollup — committed vs. planned

The rollup buckets the client's roadmap investment against their annual budget:

- **Committed** — roadmap items that are `in_progress` or `completed`. The client
  has approved this work; it's underway or done.
- **Planned** — items still in `planned` status. Proposed, not yet approved.
- **Excluded** — `deferred` and `cancelled` items are left out of the rollup
  entirely.

The **FY investment total** is committed + planned, and the **headroom** is the
annual budget minus that total. Go past the budget and the rollup flags it as
over budget. Investment is also broken out **by quarter**, using the target
quarter you set on each [roadmap item](/composer/technology-roadmap#managing-the-roadmap).

## The QBR deliverable

`/summit/qbr?company={id}` assembles the client's business review from everything
Summit already knows — nothing to re-enter:

- The current **alignment score**, from the client's latest review.
- The **budget rollup** — committed, planned, headroom, and the by-quarter
  breakdown.
- The **technology roadmap** with per-item cost and **provenance chips** showing
  which Standard and control each item traces back to.
- The **accepted-risk register** — the risks the client chose not to remediate,
  each with its required re-review date.

Open `/summit/qbr` without a client to get a picker. The QBR renders on-brand and
tablet-friendly, and a **Print / Save PDF** action isolates the deliverable from
the app chrome so you can hand the client a clean document.

## Signing off the review

The alignment review that feeds the QBR carries its own gated sign-off chain — the
TAM signs off the assessment, the vCIO countersigns, and the client signs off last
during the walkthrough, which is what seals the review as complete and finalizes
every deferred risk. Every sign-off is a real drawn signature written to a
tenant-scoped audit trail, so the QBR is backed by a signed, audited record. The
full chain, the attestation lock, and the vCIO round-trip are covered on
[Technology Alignment reviews](/composer/alignment-reviews#the-guided-workflow);
the on-device client experience is on
[Client review walkthrough](/composer/client-walkthrough).

<Note>
  The QBR is currently an internal, printable page. A read-only client-portal
  mirror of the alignment score and roadmap is planned.
</Note>
