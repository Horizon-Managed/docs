---

Client experience surveys let you ask a client's contact one simple question — how satisfied they are with your service — and turn the answers into a sentiment signal on the account. You generate a **single-use CSAT link** for a specific contact, send it to them however you like (email, SMS, a chat message), and their rating flows straight back into the client's health picture. No template designer, no invite campaign, no extra tooling: pick a contact, generate a link, paste it, done.

You generate and manage these links from the **Account Mgmt** tab of a client in the CRM. The client fills out the survey at a public link of the form `/s/<token>`.

<Note>
This is **not** the same feature as **Site Assessments** (the iPad site-walkthrough tool, on its own tab of the client's detail page). Client experience surveys measure how a client *feels* about your service; Site Assessments capture a physical site for a bid. They are unrelated systems.
</Note>

## When to use it

- After you close a significant ticket and want to confirm the client is happy with how it went.
- Following an onboarding, a project milestone, or a quarterly business review.
- When an account feels quiet and you want an explicit read on sentiment before it becomes a renewal risk.
- Any time you want a client-authored data point feeding the **Sentiment** gauge on the client's header.

## How it works

Each survey link is tied to **one contact** and is **single-use**. The first person to submit a rating consumes the link; after that, the link shows a "no longer valid" page to anyone else who opens it. You generate a fresh link per person you want to hear from.

<Note>
Because the link is single-use, pasting it into a broad channel (a group email, a shared chat) is **first-responder-wins** — the first submission consumes it and everyone else sees the "no longer valid" page. Send each link to one known contact.
</Note>

The survey itself is **CSAT only**: a single question rated **1 to 5**, plus an optional free-text comment. Because you already chose the recipient when you generated the link, the public page never asks the client for their name or email — it already knows who they are and which company they belong to.

## Generate a survey link

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the client">
    In the CRM, open the client, then select the **Account Mgmt** tab. The CSAT survey section lives there alongside the account's other health tools.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the contact">
    Choose the contact you want to survey from the **contact** dropdown. The link is attributed to that person, so their response is recorded against them. If the client has no contacts yet, add one first — you can't generate a link without a recipient.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate the link">
    Select **Generate link**. A new single-use link appears in the active-links list below, showing the contact, the survey type, the link itself, and when it was created.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy and send">
    Use **Copy link** to put the URL on your clipboard, then paste it into whatever channel you already use with that contact — a direct email, an SMS, a chat DM. There is no built-in send; delivery is up to you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Manage active links

The active-links list shows every link that hasn't been deactivated, newest first. A link stays in this list even after it has been used — its single-use protection is enforced by the token itself, not by removing the row, so a spent link simply shows the "no longer valid" page if anyone opens it again. Each row gives you:

| Column | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Contact** | The person the link is attributed to. |
| **Type** | The survey type (`CSAT`). |
| **Survey link** | The public URL, ready to copy. |
| **Created** | The date the link was generated. |
| **Deactivate** | Manually retires the link. |

Selecting **Deactivate** asks you to confirm, then retires the link immediately — the contact can no longer use it, and it drops out of the active-links list. There is **no automatic expiry**: a link stays live until it is either used once or deactivated by hand. Deactivate a link if you sent it to the wrong person, or if it's gone stale and you'd rather it stopped working.

## What the client sees

The public link opens a standalone, branded page — your company's identity up top, a small "Powered by NEXOS CORE" mark in the footer, no login, no app chrome. It greets the contact by first name and names their company, then asks one question:

> **Overall, how satisfied were you with the support we provided?**

The client picks a face on a **1 to 5** scale, from **Very dissatisfied** (1) through **Neutral** (3) to **Very satisfied** (5), and can optionally add a comment (up to 2,000 characters). After they submit, they see a short thank-you that names their company. If they open a link that has already been used or been deactivated, they instead see a friendly "this survey link is no longer valid" page telling them to reach out for a fresh one.

## The sentiment gauge

Once responses land, they roll up into the **Sentiment** gauge in the client's header posture strip, next to compliance, SLA, and touches.

The gauge is the client's **all-time average CSAT score, normalized to 0–100** — the average rating divided by 5, times 100. So an average rating of 4.0 shows as **80**, and 3.0 shows as **60**. Every scored CSAT response counts; there is **no recency window**.

The colour tracks the score:

| Score | Colour | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 80 and above | Green | Happy |
| 60 to 79 | Amber | Watch |
| Below 60 | Red | At-risk |

Until at least one CSAT response is submitted, the gauge shows **n/a** rather than a misleading zero — an empty account reads as "no signal yet," not "unhappy."

## Audit trail

Every survey event is recorded to an append-only audit log so there's a defensible record of who did what:

- A link being **generated** (which staff member, which contact).
- A link being **deactivated**.
- A response being **captured** (attributed to the responding contact, with the score).

The audit trail deliberately never stores the raw link token or the client's free-text comment — link events record only a short, non-secret token prefix for correlation, and the comment is feedback that lives with the response, not in the audit record. These entries are immutable once written.

## Good to know

- **CSAT only for now.** The survey is a single 1–5 satisfaction question. Other survey types (NPS, onboarding, QBR, annual) are planned but not yet available to send.
- **Delivery is manual.** There is no invite email or automated campaign — you copy the link and send it yourself.
- **Attribution is per contact.** Each link, and each response, is tied to the specific contact you chose.
- **Single-use is the point.** One link equals one captured response; generate a new link for each recipient.

## How it connects

Client experience surveys sit inside the [CRM](/crm/index) and surface on the client's detail page, feeding the same header posture strip that shows compliance and SLA health. Pair them with a resolved [ticket](/helpdesk/ticket-queue) to confirm the client was satisfied with how you handled it — a quick CSAT link after a big fix turns a closed ticket into a measured sentiment signal on the account.
